# Current breadboard circuit

State on 8 September 2026, after phase 3 was closed. This document replaces
the earlier state snapshots and must be updated with every change on the
breadboard. Every detail was confirmed by eye on the breadboard on
8 September 2026.

## What the circuit does

The ESP32-C6 board generates the DCC waveform and feeds it to the DRV8874
H-bridge, which drives the two rails. After every packet addressed to a
locomotive the board opens a RailCom cutout: for about 460 microseconds the
bridge brakes, that is it shorts the two rails together, and the decoder in
the locomotive answers with small current pulses. A resistor in series with
each rail turns those pulses into millivolts, an LM339 comparator turns them
into logic levels and the board's serial port reads them on GPIO5.

The reference measurements of this circuit are the oscilloscope captures of
7 September 2026, files WAVE1-4 in the `OneDrive/dcc` folder, and the
board's serial log of the same day. How the firmware behaves around the
cutout is described in `docs/specs/rmt-isr-dcc-railcom-flow.md`.

## Power supply

A single star ground, where every return goes: the board, the bridge, the
logic chips and the comparator. There are three supply lines: the 15 V from
the bench supply, which enters only the VIN pin of the Pololu module; the
5 V for the 74HC08, the 74HC14 and the LM339, taken from the USB connector
of the ESP32-C6 board and therefore present only while the USB cable is
plugged in; and the board's 3.3 V, used only for the pull-up of the
comparator output. On the printed board the 5 V will have to come from a
converter fed by the 15 V. The VM pin of the module must not be connected:
it is a measurement point after the protection, not an input.

## The board and its pins

GPIO2 carries the DCC waveform generated by the RMT peripheral. GPIO4 is
the run/cutout signal: high during normal transmission, low during the
RailCom cutout. GPIO18 wakes the bridge and is held low until the firmware
is ready. GPIO5 is the serial input that receives the decoder's answer,
with the internal pull-up enabled. GPIO3 is the short-circuit detector
input. GPIO19 and GPIO20 are the display data and clock. GPIO21 and GPIO22
read the resume and stop buttons. GPIO14 and GPIO15 drive the green and red
LEDs.

## The bridge and the brake logic

The DRV8874 works in PWM mode: its PMODE pin is at 3.3 V. In this mode the
two inputs IN1 and IN2 drive the two outputs directly, and with both inputs
high the bridge brakes.

The two inputs do not come from the board directly; they pass through two
AND gates of the 74HC08 and two inverters of the 74HC14. The first AND gate
receives the DCC waveform from GPIO2 and the run signal from GPIO4; the
second receives the DCC waveform inverted by one 74HC14 inverter and the
same run signal. The gate outputs, pins 3 and 6 of the 74HC08, enter two
74HC14 inverters, pins 5 and 9, and their outputs, pins 6 and 8, go to the
bridge inputs crosswise: IN1 from pin 8, IN2 from pin 6. As a result,
outside the cutout the bridge reproduces the DCC waveform as before, and
inside the cutout, with GPIO4 low, both inputs rise and the bridge brakes.
Measured on 1 September: during the cutout both inputs sit at about 3.2 V
for 460 microseconds and the rails are at zero.

The FAULT pin of the bridge is not connected to the board: the firmware's
short-circuit detector on GPIO3 does not see the bridge today, and the
filter that sat on GPIO3 in May is gone. On the run signal, between GPIO4
and the 74HC08, a resistor to ground keeps the bridge inputs at rest while
the board is not yet driving the pin; the design value is 100 kΩ.

## The sense resistors

Between the bridge output and each of the two rails sits a sense resistor:
two 4.7 Ω resistors in parallel, about 2.35 Ω. Across each one, two 1N5819
diodes in anti-parallel let the traction current through and leave the much
smaller decoder current alone. Both rails are equipped the same way,
confirmed on 8 September 2026.

The decoder current, about 30 mA, produces about 70 mV across each
resistor; its direction depends on how the locomotive sits on the track,
which is why the comparator watches both rails. Outside the cutout the two
nodes swing with the DCC waveform and the diodes keep them within half a
volt.

## The comparator

The LM339 is powered at 5 V on pin 3, with ground on pin 12 and a 100 nF
capacitor between the two. The threshold is a voltage of about 18.5 mV
obtained from a divider between the 5 V and ground, 22 kΩ plus 4.7 kΩ on top
and 100 Ω at the bottom, brought to pins 5 and 7. It corresponds to 8 mA
through the sense resistor, inside the band that RCN-217 requires of the
detector.

The rail-side node of the first sense resistor reaches pin 4 and that of
the second reaches pin 6, each through a 1 kΩ resistor that protects the
chip while those nodes swing by tens of volts outside the cutout.

The two outputs, pins 1 and 2, are tied together: they are open-collector
and can only pull low. From the common node a 1 kΩ resistor goes up to the
3.3 V, not to the 5 V, so GPIO5 never sees more than 3.3 V. The same node
goes to GPIO5.

The capacitors in the whole circuit are four, all 100 nF, one per chip on
its supply: 74HC08, 74HC14, LM339 and the ESP32-C6 board.

There is no hysteresis. The comparator chatters for two or three
microseconds around slow edges, in particular when the residual locomotive
current falls below the threshold at the start of the cutout; the serial
port is not affected because it samples in the middle of each bit.

## What to expect when measuring

On the GPIO5 pin, referred to ground: the line is low outside the cutout,
because the traction current keeps the comparator conducting; at the start
of the cutout the line stays low for about twenty more microseconds while
the residual current dies out, then rises and stays high; from the
decoder's first bit onwards, pulses to ground of 4 microseconds each. In
the captures of 7 September the decoder's first bit arrives 84 microseconds
after the end of the packet and the line is high and steady from
49 microseconds on.

## What is no longer there

The BTS7960 bridge of the first build, the two TLV3501 comparators, the
external terminator with the IRLZ44N MOSFETs and their burden resistors, and
the 74HC14 inverter on the board's serial input, which served an earlier
front end and is now replaced by the pull-up to 3.3 V.
