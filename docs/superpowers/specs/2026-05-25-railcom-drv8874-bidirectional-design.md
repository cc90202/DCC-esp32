# RailCom Detector — DRV8874 Bidirectional Design

Date: 2026-05-25
Status: Superseded by 2026-05-28 electrical review; cutout decision retained,
detector front-end unsafe as written

> **2026-05-28 correction:** the DRV8874 brake-low cutout decision remains
> valid, but the detector implementation below is not safe as a build guide.
> The TLV3501 inputs must not be tied directly to rail-side shunt nodes unless
> the front-end also handles the normal DCC common-mode voltage outside the
> cutout. Use this document only for the cutout/firmware invariants until the
> detector front-end is redesigned.
>
> **Replacement build direction:** use
> [`docs/hardware/railcom-option-a-external-terminator.md`](../../hardware/railcom-option-a-external-terminator.md)
> for the next RailCom rebuild. That design moves the DRV8874 to PWM mode and
> uses a cutout-only external terminator/detector.

## Overview

This document specifies the hardware architecture and firmware changes needed
to implement RailCom reception on the DCC command station now that the
Pololu DRV8874 has replaced the BTS7960 as the H-bridge.

**Companion document:** the concrete wiring guide, pin-by-pin checklists, and
component values for the same architecture live in
[`docs/hardware/drv8874-railcom-circuit.md`](../../hardware/drv8874-railcom-circuit.md).
That document is the canonical reference when building the breadboard. This
document explains the architectural decisions and validation strategy behind
those choices.

It supersedes:

- the parts of [`docs/superpowers/plans/2026-03-31-railcom-design.md`](../plans/2026-03-31-railcom-design.md)
  that assume a BTS7960 booster (the firmware architecture decisions in that
  document still apply);
- the breadboard fixtures documented in `docs/hardware/railcom-detector-v3-breadboard-layout.md`
  and `docs/hardware/railcom-detector-v4-bidirectional-fix.md`, which used
  external cutout MOSFETs as a workaround for BTS7960 limitations.

The new architecture finally honors the original design intent of the
2026-03-31 plan — *no external cutout MOSFETs in the sensing path* — because
the DRV8874 provides native rail-shorting behavior through its internal
low-side FETs that the BTS7960 lacked.

## Background

The 2026-03-31 plan rejected the previous "diode bridge + 4.7 Ω burden + MOSFET"
topology as the production architecture, requiring instead:

- rails physically shorted together at low impedance during cutout,
- detector burden within the RailCom limits (≤ 200 mV at 34 mA),
- cutout add ≤ 10 mV at 34 mA.

On the BTS7960 this requirement could not be met natively: when the chip is
disabled it tri-states the outputs, it does not short them. The v3/v4
breadboards therefore kept external MOSFETs as a workaround. RailCom
reception on that fixture worked only as a sporadic proof of concept,
with about one successful POM CV read every 200 seconds against a real
LokSound 5 decoder (see
[`project_railcom_loksound_diagnosis`](../../../.claude/projects/-home-crist-DCC-esp32/memory/project_railcom_loksound_diagnosis.md)).

The DRV8874 (verified at the bench 2026-05-24, see
[`docs/hardware/drv8874-bench-checklist.md`](../../hardware/drv8874-bench-checklist.md))
exposes a fast `EN` pin that, when driven LOW in PH/EN mode, places both
outputs at GND through internal low-side FETs (brake-low, approximately
50 mΩ per side). This delivers the rail short the original design wanted,
without external switches.

## Goal

Implement bidirectional RailCom reception on the DRV8874 bench setup with:

- single global detector across both rails (no track sectioning),
- both current directions sensed (CH1 ACK, CH1 address, CH2 datagrams
  including CV reads),
- cutout generated by the H-bridge itself (no external MOSFETs),
- safety semantics around `GPIO18` preserved (master enable only, never
  toggled by the cutout sequence).

## Architecture Decisions

### Decision 1 — Cutout via DRV8874 brake-low, not external MOSFETs

Drive the DRV8874 `EN/IN1` pin from the firmware cutout signal `GPIO4`
directly. Stay in PH/EN mode (`PMODE = GND` unchanged).

Rationale: per the TI DRV8874 datasheet truth table, `EN = LOW` in PH/EN
mode produces brake-low on both outputs (approximately 50 mΩ each side
to GND), which is exactly the low-impedance rail short the 2026-03-31
plan required during the cutout window. External cutout MOSFETs become
unnecessary.

### Decision 2 — Bidirectional sense with two TLV3501 comparators

Place a small fixed shunt resistor in series with each rail and sense
each shunt with its own TLV3501 comparator. Combine the two push-pull
outputs through a diode-OR network into a single active-low RailCom
data line.

Rationale: the RailCom current pulses from the decoder transmitter can
flow in either direction through the rail-to-rail brake loop, depending
on the decoder design and the DCC polarity at cutout time. Sensing a
single rail (the v3 topology) catches only half of the directionally
asymmetric data, as documented in
[`docs/hardware/railcom-detector-v4-bidirectional-fix.md`](../../hardware/railcom-detector-v4-bidirectional-fix.md).
The inventory now has two TLV3501 comparators available
(see [`docs/hardware/components-inventory.md`](../../hardware/components-inventory.md)),
matching the v4 design exactly.

### Decision 3 — Start without filter or hysteresis on the TLV3501

The comparator front-end starts with the bare topology: inverting input
straight from the sense node, non-inverting input straight from the
threshold reference, no series filter, no hysteresis feedback.

Rationale: on the BTS, the sub-microsecond glitches at the sense node
forced us to add a `220 Ω + 4.7 nF` filter and `300 kΩ` hysteresis to
get usable edges (see
[`project_railcom_loksound_diagnosis`](../../../.claude/projects/-home-crist-DCC-esp32/memory/project_railcom_loksound_diagnosis.md)).
The BTS produced those glitches because of its slow, noisy switch-off
behavior. The DRV8874 switches cleanly into brake-low in microseconds
and is expected to deliver cleaner sense edges that the TLV3501 can
read directly.

If Bench Gate 1 or Bench Gate 2 shows glitch-driven false transitions
or degraded reception, fall back to the BTS-era sweet spot
(`220 Ω + 4.7 nF` filter and `300 kΩ` hysteresis per comparator) as a
documented escape hatch. Re-tune one variable at a time per the prior
diagnosis history.

### Decision 4 — Firmware invariant: cutout never touches the master enable

The cutout state machine in `src/track_output.rs` must not modify `GPIO18`
in any branch, including error-recovery branches. Only the fault-manager
path (via `TrackOutput::set_track_enabled`) is allowed to move `GPIO18`.

Rationale: on the DRV8874, `GPIO18` drives `nSLEEP`, which has a 1 ms
wake/sleep transition time per the TI datasheet — far too slow to fit
inside a 460 µs RailCom cutout window. Toggling `nSLEEP` during the
cutout sequence would either truncate the cutout, leave the chip
unresponsive, or generate timing glitches. The fast cutout is produced
entirely by the `EN` pin driven from `GPIO4`.

## Hardware Design

### Cutout signal path

The cutout signal flows from the firmware to the DRV8874 with no
intermediate logic gates:

```
GPIO4 (idle HIGH, cutout LOW) ────────────► DRV8874 EN/IN1
```

The previous 74HC14 inverter between `GPIO4` and the now-deleted external
MOSFETs is no longer used in the signal path. The polarity of `GPIO4`
matches `EN/IN1` directly: HIGH idle (chip drives DCC normally), LOW
during cutout (brake-low, both outputs to GND).

`GPIO18` continues to drive `nSLEEP` as the master enable; the bench
wiring around `nSLEEP` (verified 2026-05-24) is unchanged.

### Sense network

Two TLV3501 comparators, one per rail, with diode-OR combining. The
canonical values are documented in
[`docs/hardware/drv8874-railcom-circuit.md`](../../hardware/drv8874-railcom-circuit.md);
they are reproduced here for reference and rationale.

| Reference | Value | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `R_A`, `R_B` | 2.7 Ω, ≥ 1 W metal film | In series between each DRV8874 output and the corresponding rail |
| `R_ref_top` | 150 kΩ, 1% | Threshold divider high side |
| `R_ref_bot` | 1 kΩ, 1% | Threshold divider low side |
| `C_ref` | 100 nF ceramic | `VREF_RAILCOM` decoupling to GND |
| `D_or_A`, `D_or_B` | 1N5819 Schottky | Diode-OR combining from each comparator output |
| `R_or_pullup` | 4.7 kΩ | Pull-up on the combined `RAILCOM_RAW` node, to 3.3 V |

Threshold calculation:

```
VREF_RAILCOM = 3.3 V × 1 kΩ / (150 kΩ + 1 kΩ) ≈ 21.9 mV
I_trip = VREF_RAILCOM / R_sense = 21.9 mV / 2.7 Ω ≈ 8.1 mA
```

This trip point sits in the middle of the NMRA RailCom detection region
(logic 0 above 10 mA, logic 1 below 6 mA per S-9.3.2), giving symmetric
margin on both sides.

Burden budget at peak DCC current (1 A through a single rail, two-loco
typical or four-loco transient): `1 A × 2.7 Ω = 2.7 V` drop and `2.7 W`
dissipation per resistor. The 1 W metal-film parts in inventory are
adequate for the bench bring-up with two H0 locomotives; for larger
loads, reconsider `R_sense` or move to higher-power parts.

Burden during cutout at 30 mA RailCom current: `30 mA × 2.7 Ω = 81 mV`
across each sense resistor, well within the NMRA S-9.3.2 detector budget
of 200 mV at 34 mA.

Per Decision 3, the analog front-end starts without the BTS-era filter
and hysteresis components. They remain available as a documented
fallback if Bench Gate 1 or Gate 2 shows glitches or degraded reception.

### Diode-OR combining

Each TLV3501 idles HIGH (no current through its sense resistor) and pulls
LOW when its rail's burden exceeds threshold. The combining works as
follows:

- `D_or_A` cathode at TLV3501-A OUT, anode at the combining node;
- `D_or_B` cathode at TLV3501-B OUT, anode at the combining node;
- `R_or_pullup` from the combining node to 3.3 V.

The combining node idles HIGH (no diode conducts, pull-up holds HIGH).
When either comparator drives LOW, its diode conducts and pulls the
combining node to about `0.3 V` (Schottky forward drop above the
comparator's saturated LOW output), which the 74HC14 reads as LOW.

The combining node `RAILCOM_RAW` feeds **one single** gate of the 74HC14
used as a clean-edge inverting buffer; that gate's output drives `GPIO5`.

### UART RX pin and polarity

`GPIO5` is the RailCom RX pin. The current firmware UART configuration
expects the signal **already inverted** at the GPIO (UART idle level
matches the comparator idle plus one inversion from the 74HC14), which
is why exactly one 74HC14 gate sits between `RAILCOM_RAW` and `GPIO5`,
not two and not zero.

### Power and ground

`VIN` of the DRV8874 takes the 15 V bench supply directly, unchanged
from the verified DCC base wiring. Logic 3.3 V comes from the ESP32-C6
board regulator.

A single star ground point near the DRV8874 `GND` pin collects:

- the GND side of both `R_sense`,
- the `VREF` divider lower leg and decoupling cap,
- both TLV3501 ground pins,
- both filter capacitors `C_filt_*`.

The 74HC14 logic ground returns to the ESP32-C6 ground through the
existing breadboard ground rail. This keeps the small RailCom signal
isolated from the high-current booster ground loop.

## Firmware Changes

### Scope

Changes are localized to `src/track_output.rs`. No other firmware module
is affected; the RMT engine, the cutout state machine logic, the UART
reception path, the RailCom parser, the POM actor, and the Z21 integration
all remain as-is.

### Concrete edits

The principle in Decision 4 translates to:

1. In `request_cutout_from_isr`, remove the call to `enable_low_fast(hw)`
   at the start of the cutout sequence. The chip stays awake; only the
   `cutout_off_fast` / `cutout_on_fast` calls (which drive `GPIO4`) operate.

2. In `cutout_timer_interrupt`, in every error-recovery branch across
   `CUTOUT_STATE_WAITING_TO_OPEN`, `CUTOUT_STATE_WAITING_CH1_OPEN`,
   `CUTOUT_STATE_CH1_OPEN`, `CUTOUT_STATE_WAITING_CH2_OPEN`,
   `CUTOUT_STATE_CH2_OPEN`, and `CUTOUT_STATE_WAITING_TO_CLOSE`, remove
   the conditional re-toggle of the enable pin:

   ```rust
   // remove this pattern in every error / completion branch:
   if TRACK_ENABLED.load(Ordering::Acquire) {
       enable_high_fast(hw);
   } else {
       enable_low_fast(hw);
   }
   ```

   The cutout sequence completes by toggling only `GPIO4` (`cutout_off_fast`)
   and leaves the enable pin alone.

3. `TrackOutput::set_track_enabled` is unchanged. It remains the only
   path that drives `enable_low_fast` / `enable_high_fast` on `GPIO18`.

4. The hardware comment block in `track_output.rs` that documents the
   GPIO4 → 74HC14 inverter → external MOSFET path must be rewritten to
   reflect the new direct GPIO4 → DRV8874 `EN/IN1` wiring.

### Tests

- `cargo check-esp` and `cargo build-esp` must pass.
- `cargo clippy` clean on `src/track_output.rs`.
- Existing host tests (`cargo test-host`) must still pass; they do not
  cover ISR/hardware paths but verify the rest of the system is intact.

## Validation Plan

### Bench Gate 0 — Brake-low verification (mandatory pre-firmware)

Before committing the firmware change, verify with the oscilloscope that
pulling `EN/IN1` LOW on the actual Pololu carrier produces brake-low
(both `OUT1` and `OUT2` to GND with low-impedance sink), not coast
(both outputs in Hi-Z).

Procedure:

- DRV8874 powered (15 V on `VIN`, 3.3 V on logic side, `nSLEEP` HIGH).
- No DCC signal: leave `PH/IN2` tied to GND or to 3.3 V (static).
- Manually toggle `EN/IN1` between 3.3 V and GND using a jumper wire.
- Probe `OUT1` and `OUT2` with the scope referenced to system GND.

Acceptance:

- With `EN = LOW`: both outputs at less than 50 mV with respect to GND,
  with measurable sink capability (briefly pull one output to 3.3 V
  through a 1 kΩ resistor and verify the chip pulls it back).
- With `EN = HIGH`: outputs follow `PH/IN2` normally.

If brake-low is not produced, this design is invalid. Fall back to one
of:

- IN/IN mode (`PMODE = 3.3 V`, two GPIOs for `IN1`/`IN2`, diode-OR
  network in front of the chip — a larger firmware change);
- external MOSFETs (the previously rejected approach).

### Bench Gate 1 — Detector electrical validation

After building the sense network and committing the firmware change,
validate on the scope:

1. DCC waveform on rails: no degradation in edge quality or amplitude
   from the 0.5 Ω in-line shunts.
2. Differential rail voltage during cutout window: close to zero, with
   well-defined low-impedance sink to GND.
3. Cutout start delay from the last DCC packet edge: 29 µs deadtime per
   `CUTOUT_START_DEADTIME_US` in `src/track_output.rs`, matching NMRA
   S-9.3.2.
4. Voltage across `R_sense_A` and `R_sense_B` during cutout: pulse
   activity visible when a RailCom-capable decoder sits on the rails.
5. Comparator output rise and fall times at the diode-OR combined node:
   clean enough for 250 kbps UART reception (sub-microsecond edges
   preferred).
6. Short detector behavior on `GPIO3` unchanged: still latches correctly
   on a real short, no false trips from the new sense network.

### Bench Gate 2 — RailCom data reception

With a known RailCom-capable decoder (ESU LokSound 5 at address 3) on
the rails:

- Run `bash tools/pom_loop.sh` to repeat POM read requests of CV 8 and
  CV 29.
- Inspect `defmt` logs for `railcom pom rx:` entries containing
  `Datagram(CvData(...))`.

Acceptance:

- At least one successful POM CV read within 60 seconds of decoder
  warm-up.
- Reception rate at least double the previous BTS baseline (about one
  successful CV read per 200 seconds). Target: one per 60 seconds or
  better.
- No regression in normal DCC throughput or short-circuit detection.

## Risks

### High risk

- **Brake-low behavior not matching datasheet** on the Pololu carrier
  variant. Mitigated by Bench Gate 0 before any firmware change is
  committed.
- **In-line shunt burden during DCC**: 2.7 Ω × 1 A peak gives 2.7 V
  drop and 2.7 W dissipation per resistor. Acceptable for the 2-4 H0
  locomotive target on the bench with 1 W metal-film parts. For larger
  loads or sustained high-current operation, reconsider `R_sense` value
  or move to higher-power parts.

### Medium risk

- **Diode-OR combined LOW level**: the Schottky drop (~0.3 V) sits on
  top of the comparator's saturated LOW output. The 74HC14 input
  threshold (around 1 V on the LOW side for Schmitt trigger) provides
  margin, but validate at Bench Gate 1.
- **No analog filter / hysteresis on the comparators by default**
  (per Decision 3). If the DRV8874 turn-off transition produces residual
  ringing on the sense nodes, the TLV3501 may catch glitches and produce
  spurious UART edges. The documented escape hatch is to add the BTS-era
  sweet spot (`220 Ω + 4.7 nF` filter and `300 kΩ` hysteresis per
  comparator) and re-validate.
- **DRV8874 internal current sense path** (`CS` / `IPROPI` pin) is
  unused in this design but still electrically present. If the short
  detector on `GPIO3` shows false trips during cutouts, investigate
  whether the brake-mode current pulses on the low-side FETs leak into
  the `CS` measurement.

### Low risk

- **Firmware refactor in `track_output.rs`**: changes are localized,
  preserve existing public API, and do not touch RMT timing or the
  cutout state machine sequencing.

## Open Questions

- Should `R_sense` be smaller (1.5 Ω, matching the mtb-rc reference)
  to reduce DCC burden, at the cost of a smaller RailCom signal?
  Decide after Bench Gate 1 measurements at 10 mA, 30 mA, and 34 mA
  cutout currents.
- Should the threshold reference use a trimmer instead of a fixed
  divider for easier in-field tuning? Probably yes once the topology
  is validated; defer to a v2 of this design.
- Should the filter and hysteresis be added preemptively at build time
  rather than as a fallback? Decision deferred to Bench Gate 1 results.

## Implementation Sequence

1. Bench Gate 0: manual brake-low verification on the existing DRV8874
   bench setup (no firmware change yet).
2. Apply the firmware edits to `src/track_output.rs`. Run
   `cargo check-esp`, `cargo build-esp`, `cargo clippy`,
   `cargo test-host`.
3. Flash the new firmware. Confirm with the scope that the cutout window
   on the rails is now produced by brake-low (rails at GND during the
   460 µs window, no chip sleep).
4. Build the sense network: in-line shunts on both rails, threshold
   divider, two TLV3501 stages with filter and hysteresis, diode-OR,
   74HC14 buffer.
5. Bench Gate 1: oscilloscope validation of the analog and digital
   waveforms.
6. Wire the UART RX pin and run Bench Gate 2 against a real RailCom
   decoder.
7. Update [`docs/hardware/drv8874-bench-checklist.md`](../../hardware/drv8874-bench-checklist.md)
   with the verified RailCom additions, and close the historical
   v3/v4 docs by marking them as superseded.
